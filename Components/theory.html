Two-Factor Theory

Frederick Herzberg was an American psychologist who studied what makes people feel good or bad about their jobs. His Two-Factor Theory, also called the Motivation-Hygiene Theory, came out of that research.

Herzberg proposed that job satisfaction and dissatisfaction are caused by different things. They are not opposite ends of one scale.

Motivators such as achievement, recognition and growth make people satisfied. Hygiene factors such as pay, policies and working conditions only stop people from being unhappy.

The Two Factors

Motivators

They lift people up

Achievement, recognition, meaningful work, responsibility and room to grow. When these are present, people feel satisfied and want to do their best work.

Satisfaction

Hygiene factors

They prevent unhappiness

Pay, company policies, working conditions and fair treatment. When these are missing, people become dissatisfied. Having them only removes that problem and does not motivate by itself.

Why We Chose It

Our team needs both fair working conditions and genuinely meaningful work to thrive.

Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory reminds us that clear policies and fair pay prevent dissatisfaction. Open recognition, trust, and opportunities for growth are what truly motivate people to do their best work.

Using both sides together helps us keep the group running smoothly while keeping every member inspired.
